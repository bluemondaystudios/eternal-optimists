How we teach

Our youngest learners grow through play using the national early learning framework. From Grade R, learners follow CAPS, the national school curriculum.

Toddlers & Pre-school

NCF: Learning through play

We follow the National Curriculum Framework (NCF) for early childhood. Children learn through play, routines, stories and songs, guided by six Early Learning and Development Areas (ELDAs).

  • A safe, clean and loving environment
  • Qualified and caring teachers
  • Daily activities, stories, songs, art and outdoor play

The 6 learning areas (ELDAs)

  1. 1
    Well-being

    Health, safety, eating, sleeping, washing hands and moving our bodies.

  2. 2
    Identity and Belonging

    Who am I? My family, my culture, my language and feeling loved.

  3. 3
    Communication

    Talking, listening, songs, stories and learning our home language.

  4. 4
    Exploring Mathematics

    Counting, shapes, colours, big and small, sorting and patterns.

  5. 5
    Creativity

    Drawing, painting, music, dancing and pretend play.

  6. 6
    Knowledge and Understanding of the World

    Nature, animals, weather, water, sand and our community.

Grade R to Grade 2

CAPS: The national curriculum

From Grade R, learners follow CAPS (the Curriculum and Assessment Policy Statement), the national curriculum set by the Department of Basic Education for Grade R to Grade 12. Grade R to Grade 3 is the Foundation Phase, which builds the reading, writing and number skills every learner needs for big school.

Foundation Phase subjects

  • Home Language: English

    Listening, speaking, reading and writing in English, with phonics and handwriting built step by step.

  • First Additional Language: Sepedi

    Learners grow in Sepedi, starting with listening and speaking and building up to reading and writing.

  • Mathematics

    Numbers, counting and calculating, patterns, shapes, measurement and working with data.

  • Life Skills

    Beginning Knowledge, Creative Arts, Physical Education, and Personal and Social Well-being.

In Grade R much of this learning still happens through play, so the step up to Grade 1 feels natural.
